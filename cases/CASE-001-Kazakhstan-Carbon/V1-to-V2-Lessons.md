# Kazakhstan MOU V1.0 → V2.0 Lessons

## Lesson 1 — MOU Is Not a Mini Final Contract
V1.0 tended to solve too many future issues too early.

V2.0 focused on:
- cooperation purpose
- contributions
- commercial logic
- planning assumptions
- next stage

General rule:
Do not solve 20 years of legal and operational detail inside an early-stage MOU.

## Lesson 2 — US$10/ha Was Better Than a Large Lump-Sum Figure
A large project amount could be interpreted as a consulting fee.

The per-hectare development-budget model:
- matched the land-based nature of the project
- scaled with actual project area
- made the economics easier to understand
- reduced the vendor-fee perception

General rule:
Find the project's natural economic unit.

## Lesson 3 — Gross Revenue Must Not Be Called Profit
Planning logic:
500,000 ha × 3 tCO2e/ha/year × US$20/tCO2e
= approximately US$30M/year potential carbon-credit sales revenue.

It is not:
- net profit
- Party A's income
- ZLSS income

General rule:
Label every financial number by its true economic meaning.

## Lesson 4 — Protect Know-how
Red-team questions about:
- cost breakdown
- sampling design
- MRV
- technical architecture
- first-90-day execution

were legitimate questions, but not all should be answered before NDA.

General rule:
Before MOU, prove capability and commercial logic.
After NDA, disclose technical detail on a need-to-know basis.

## Lesson 5 — Positive Commercial Language Outperformed Defensive Drafting
V1.0 included more defensive wording.
V2.0 replaced much of it with positive planning language.

General rule:
At MOU stage, use wording that supports cooperation while preserving precision.

## Lesson 6 — Conservative Assumptions Build Credibility
Prior team experience may support a higher figure, but the project planning basis used a more conservative assumption.

General rule:
Use evidence-backed conservative planning assumptions instead of aggressive promises.

## Lesson 7 — Policy Alignment Is a Value Layer, Not a Guarantee
National climate-policy alignment can strengthen project relevance.
It must not be presented as guaranteed government support or automatic regulatory recognition.

General rule:
Distinguish policy alignment from legal entitlement or guaranteed government support.

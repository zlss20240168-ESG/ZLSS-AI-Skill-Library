# ZLSS MOU Skill v1.0

## Mission
Create commercially effective MOUs that help both parties understand the cooperation logic, see the value, and move confidently to the next stage without overloading the MOU with final-contract detail.

## Core Principle
A good MOU is not a shortened final contract.
Its job is to establish:
- what both parties want to do together,
- what each party contributes,
- why the cooperation is commercially meaningful,
- what the next step is.

## Mandatory Workflow

### 1. Identify the Deal Architecture Before Drafting
Do not start writing the MOU until the transaction is classified.

Possible structures include:
- Consulting / Service
- Joint Development
- Asset Development
- Investment
- Joint Venture
- Technology Licensing
- Distribution / Agency
- Strategic Cooperation
- Other

The wording, money logic, rights logic, and risk logic must follow the actual deal architecture.

### 2. Find the Natural Unit Economics
When a large total number may create resistance or confusion, identify a natural commercial unit.

Examples:
- per hectare
- per ton
- per site
- per unit
- per MW
- per project
- per transaction

The purpose is not to hide total cost. It is to make the economic logic understandable and scalable.

### 3. Separate Financial Concepts
Never confuse:
- Development Budget
- Service Fee
- Investment
- Revenue
- Gross Sales Revenue
- Profit
- Net Profit
- Cash Flow
- Party A Income
- Party B Income

All numbers must be labeled precisely.

### 4. Protect Know-how by Stage
Pre-MOU:
- Prove capability
- Explain commercial logic
- Show relevant track record
- Define cooperation intent
- Ask only for minimum data needed for initial judgment

Do not disclose:
- proprietary methodology logic
- detailed sampling strategy
- internal cost structure
- model parameters
- MRV architecture
- supplier pricing
- internal workflow

Post-NDA:
- disclose technical detail on a need-to-know basis

Formal Agreement:
- funding mechanism
- revenue sharing
- ownership
- development rights
- sales rights
- default
- dispute resolution
- detailed obligations

### 5. Keep the MOU Commercially Positive
Avoid defensive legal wording when a simpler positive formulation can achieve the same purpose.

Prefer:
- planning basis
- preliminary estimate
- subject to actual project data
- to be further agreed in the formal agreement

Avoid unnecessary early-stage phrases such as:
- does not constitute a guarantee
- does not pre-lock
- major additional fees
- shall not be deemed

### 6. Use Evidence Without Turning Planning Assumptions Into Guarantees
When using forecasts:
- state the assumption
- state the basis
- keep revenue distinct from profit
- leave final confirmation to actual data, science, methodology, and third-party verification where applicable

### 7. Keep the MOU Short Enough to Encourage Progress
If a clause is primarily for:
- implementation manual
- technical specification
- detailed budget appendix
- final legal risk allocation
then it probably does not belong in the MOU.

### 8. Define the Next Action Clearly
Every MOU should make the next stage obvious.

Examples:
- data exchange
- NDA
- feasibility assessment
- technical workshop
- formal agreement
- due diligence

## Automatic Red Flags
Before delivery, check whether the draft:
- accidentally turns an asset-development deal into a consulting-fee deal
- presents gross revenue as profit
- exposes proprietary know-how too early
- contains final-contract detail that slows the relationship
- creates fear through excessive defensive wording
- lacks clear unit economics
- lacks a clear next action

## Release Standard
Do not treat the MOU as final until it passes the ZLSS 95-point evaluator.

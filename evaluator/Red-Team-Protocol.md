# ZLSS MOU Red Team Protocol

Run after the first commercially complete draft and before freeze.

## Attack 1 — Counterparty CEO
Ask:
- Why should I do this?
- What do I gain without losing my existing business?
- What makes the next step worth my time?

## Attack 2 — Counterparty CFO
Ask:
- What exactly am I paying or funding?
- Is this a fee, investment, development budget, or recoverable funding?
- Are revenue and profit being confused?
- Can I understand the unit economics?

## Attack 3 — Competitor
Ask:
- Has ZLSS disclosed enough information for me to reproduce its approach?
If yes, disclosure has gone too far.

## Attack 4 — Lawyer
Ask:
- Is any essential protection missing?
- Is the document trying to solve issues that belong in the formal agreement?
Both under-lawyering and over-lawyering are defects.

## Attack 5 — Skeptical Technical Reviewer
Ask:
- Are the claims credible?
- Which numbers are facts and which are assumptions?
- Is there enough basis to continue without exposing proprietary implementation detail?

## Attack 6 — Five-Minute Test
A decision-maker should be able to explain after reading:
- what the project is
- what each party contributes
- what value may be created
- what the money means
- what happens next

## Outcome
Record:
- Critical defects
- Material improvements
- Items intentionally deferred
- Revised score

Do not add detail merely because Red Team asked a question. First decide whether that information belongs at the current disclosure stage.

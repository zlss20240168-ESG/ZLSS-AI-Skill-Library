# Common MOU Anti-Patterns

## AP-001 — Treating the MOU as a Mini Final Contract
Symptom:
Too many final legal, technical, payment, default, and risk clauses.

Why it fails:
It increases friction before both sides have even confirmed cooperation intent.

Correction:
Keep only the cooperation architecture and next-stage logic.

## AP-002 — Presenting a Large Development Budget as a Consulting Fee
Symptom:
Counterparty asks, "Why should I pay your company this amount?"

Why it fails:
The economic meaning has been framed incorrectly.

Correction:
Clarify whether the money is a project-development budget, investment, shared project cost, or service fee.

## AP-003 — Over-Disclosing Know-how
Symptom:
Technical competence is proven by giving away the technical design.

Why it fails:
The MOU becomes a free transfer of intellectual capital.

Correction:
Prove capability; disclose implementation detail only after the proper information gate.

## AP-004 — Revenue Equals Profit
Symptom:
Projected sales are described as earnings or net return.

Correction:
Label gross sales revenue, cost, and profit separately.

## AP-005 — Defensive Language Overload
Symptom:
Repeated phrases like "does not constitute", "shall not be deemed", "does not pre-lock".

Why it fails:
It makes an early-stage cooperation document feel adversarial.

Correction:
Use positive planning language and move detailed legal protections to the formal agreement.
